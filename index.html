The Internet Through Time

A year-by-year museum of how the live Web actually looked and worked — period desktop, browser chrome, signature sites, and product flows you can click through. Historical reconstruction from archives — not a modern redesign, and not a wallpaper pack.

What this museum is

  • One year at a time. Opening 2005 does not give you 2007’s iPhone or open Gmail. Products appear only when history says they should.
  • Real flows, private theater. Carts, mail, uploads, queues, and tour checks use localStorage in your browser — nothing is sent to a server.
  • Sources over vibes. Layouts, chrome, and copy track Wayback captures, period docs, and research notes in the repo — not “retro aesthetic.”

How to use the museum

  1. Pick a year — start at 1994 and walk forward, or jump to a hinge (1998 Google · 2004 Gmail · 2005 YouTube · 2007 iPhone · 2008 App Store).
  2. Connect theater — dial-up or always-on boot may play; use Skip if offered. It sets the era’s “feel,” not a real modem.
  3. Starting Point + tour — each year has a home page and short guided stops; then free-roam period sites in the content frame.
  4. Do real things — add to cart, compose mail, digg a story, build a queue: state is local only, year-prefixed so years don’t collide.
  5. Exit / resume — desktop Exit returns to this lobby. “Continue …” reopens your last year (stored as itt-last-year).

Tip: open Compare years when you wonder why 2005 has Maps but not Street View, or why 2006 is still pre-iPhone.

Privacy / theater. Carts, mail, uploads, tours, and similar state use browser localStorage only. Nothing is uploaded to a server for those flows. Clearing site data resets progress for that origin.

How years are built. Layouts and chrome track archived screenshots (Wayback and peers), browser documentation, contemporary HTML/CSS/JS capabilities, and research notes in this repo — not a generic “retro aesthetic.” Gray backgrounds, default fonts, sparse Google, broken-feeling tables, and intentional slow paths are part of the exhibit.

Accuracy bar. Products and UI motifs are year-locked. Soft “mock” buttons that only look clickable are treated as bugs; immersive actions should either work in-local or clearly refuse. Imperfections and missing pixels are preferred over inventing brand art.
