About 2008

2008 is when the phone becomes a platform and the browser is reinvented again. The App Store ships with iPhone 3G (native apps, not Safari-only). Google Chrome launches as a Windows beta built for web apps. Android reaches consumers as the T-Mobile G1. Hulu goes public; Netflix streaming densifies; Firefox 3 peaks indie browser culture. Desktop Web 2.0 is still how most people live — gatekeepers begin, they do not finish.

Scale (Internet Live Stats, June class): 172,338,726 websites (+41% from 2007’s 121,892,559) · ~1.57B internet users · chart birthmark Dropbox. Shell here: Windows XP + IE 7 mass default; Chrome is a late-year product story, not forced sole shell.

How a day felt

Boot XP · open IE 7 (or Firefox if you’re a blogger) · Digg / Facebook · Flash YouTube · open Gmail. From March: free network TV on Hulu with ads. From July: friends with iPhones talk about apps — free games, utilities, “there’s an app for that” seed culture. September: the Chrome comic leaks · try the Windows beta · multi-process tabs that don’t kill everything. Late year: if you’re on T-Mobile, the G1 is the first Android — slide keyboard, Market, “with Google.” Most of the world still lives on a fat laptop. Spotify is Europe-only invite lore — not US public.

Signature products

  • App Store — Jul 10–11 · ~500–552 apps at launch · install theater

  • Google Chrome — Sep 2 Windows beta · Dec 11 1.0 · multi-process · comic
  • T-Mobile G1 / Android — Sep unveil · Oct US retail · first consumer Android
  • Firefox 3 — Download Day culture · Jun
  • Hulu — public Mar 12 · ad-supported full episodes
  • Netflix — discs + unlimited streaming densify (not streaming-only)
  • Facebook Connect — “Log in with Facebook” begins
  • Google Friend Connect — Dec 4 public class · OpenSocial paste path
  • Dropbox — Live Stats 2008 birthmark · folder sync theater
  • Spotify Europe — Oct class · invite/freemium · not US public

Still huge (continuity)

Gmail open (since Feb 2007) · Street View (since May 2007) · Facebook Platform · Twitter · YouTube · MySpace · Digg · Wikipedia · XP still boots most PCs.
