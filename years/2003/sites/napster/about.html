About Napster (2003 looking back)

Napster was a real-time search engine for MP3 files on other users’ computers. Shawn Fanning launched it in 1999. It did not host the music — it connected sharers.

The RIAA sued in December 1999. Court orders forced filtering, then shutdown of the classic free P2P service. By 2002 the company was in bankruptcy. In 2003 the name still means “music online,” but the network people actually use is KaZaA (and friends) — while Apple’s iTunes Music Store sells legal 99¢ tracks.

Note
This page preserves the brand and the story. The classic free network is offline.
