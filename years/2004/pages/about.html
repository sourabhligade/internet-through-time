About 2004

2004 is the hinge into Web 2.0. Browser-based apps feel native (Gmail), photo sharing + tags explode (Flickr), a campus social graph seeds the future (Thefacebook), Google goes public, the first Web 2.0 Conference sells “the Web as platform,” and Firefox 1.0 (November 9) challenges IE’s peak dominance.

Scale (Internet Live Stats, June 2004): 51,611,646 websites · 910,060,180 internet users (~910M).

Four pillars in this year

  1. Gmail — Apr 1 · invite-only · 1 GB
  2. Flickr — Feb 10 · Ludicorp · tags
  3. Thefacebook — Feb 4 · Harvard / college gate
  4. Firefox 1.0 + Google IPO

Culture densify (P1/P2)

  • Web 2.0 Conference (Oct) · del.icio.us · FeedBurner
  • Digg Dec seed · Bloglines · Technorati
  • Orkut · LiveJournal · Yelp
  • craigslist · Skype · World of Warcraft (Nov)
  • BBC News · IMDb
  • MySpace mass social (~1M mid-year narrative) vs campus Thefacebook

Mass social still means MySpace — Thefacebook is campus seed, not the open web.

Anachronism bans

  • YouTube (2005)
  • Twitter (2006)
  • Open Facebook for everyone (2006) / modern Facebook product
  • Chrome browser as default
  • Yahoo already owns Flickr (Mar 2005)
  • Google Reader (2005) — Bloglines is the 2003–04 browser RSS story

Facemash (late 2003) is a footnote only — not a product room here. Sources: Cybercultural Internet 2004 · Live Stats · Mozilla/Gmail press lineage · CAPTURE-LOG Phase 9 WA.
