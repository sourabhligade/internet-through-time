June 2013 — the stack is watched

On June 5–6, 2013, reporting in The Guardian and The Washington Post brought mass surveillance programs into public view — including telephony metadata collection and the internet program known as PRISM, which documents described as involving major U.S. internet companies (Microsoft, Google, Facebook, Apple, Yahoo, and others named in public slides).

For museum visitors: this is the year privacy stops being only a specialist concern. Encryption, “who can read email,” and government access become ordinary front-page tech talk — continuing the open-web politics line after 2012’s SOPA blackout.
