NASDAQ peaks — then the long fall
March 10, 2000 — BUSINESS / MARKETS
The tech-heavy NASDAQ Composite hits its bubble peak. Months of “get big fast” meet gravity. AOL–Time Warner’s January mega-deal is still framed as triumph — crash culture accelerates through Pets.com and pink-slip lore by year end.

Judge: Microsoft holds monopoly power
November 5, 1999 — TECHNOLOGY
Judge Thomas Penfield Jackson issues Findings of Fact in the antitrust case. The ruling finds Microsoft used its Windows monopoly to crush threats including Netscape and Java. Browser bundling is at the center of the case.

Record industry sues Napster
December 6, 1999 — TECHNOLOGY / MUSIC
The RIAA files suit for contributory and vicarious copyright infringement against the peer-to-peer MP3 service. College campuses — and hard drives — are already full of free music.

Y2K: midnight approaches
SPECIAL REPORT
Governments and banks test systems as December 31 nears. Officials urge calm; chain emails urge otherwise. Will ATMs and power grids survive the rollover?

World markets ride the Web boom
BUSINESS
Portal stocks, e-commerce IPOs, and “get big fast” still dominate business pages. Yahoo owns GeoCities. Amazon sells everything. The bubble peaks March 10 then deflates — crash year culture.

Internet Explorer 5 becomes the default web
TECHNOLOGY
IE5 ships in March; Windows 98 SE follows in June. Netscape share slides. Mozilla milestones continue — but Communicator 5 never arrives in 1999.

Pop culture online: Matrix to Hampster Dance
ENTERTAINMENT
Film sites, viral GIFs, and AOL-era mass culture fill the dial-up night. Britney, Fight Club, and gaming sites pack the bookmarks folder.
