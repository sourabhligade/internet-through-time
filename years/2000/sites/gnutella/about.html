Gnutella · About

Decentralized by design

March 14, 2000: Nullsoft (Justin Frankel / Tom Pepper) releases Gnutella. AOL quickly kills the official download — then open clones proliferate across the net. Unlike Napster's central index, queries flood a peer mesh. There is no single company server to turn off.

That architecture shapes 2000–2001 file-sharing culture after Napster's court pressure: decentralize, clone, move on. College IT offices fight bandwidth the same week newspapers cover injunctions.

How it feels in 2000: install a client, connect to a few ultrapeers (later slang), search for a title, and watch a peer list populate. Transfers are best-effort. No streaming jukebox — pure P2P files.
