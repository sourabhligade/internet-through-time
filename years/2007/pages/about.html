About 2007

2007 is when the phone becomes a real web browser you carry — while most people still live on the desktop Web 2.0 stack. Apple announces and ships the iPhone (Safari + multi-touch · no App Store). Gmail opens to everyone. Street View drops you onto five US city streets. Facebook Platform turns the graph into an app mall. Twitter stops being a toy at SXSW. YouTube is Google-owned all year. Vista is on store shelves; XP + IE still boot most PCs.

Scale (Internet Live Stats, June class): 121,892,559 websites (+43% from 2006’s 85,507,314) · 1,373,327,790 internet users · chart birthmark Tumblr. Shell here: Windows XP + IE mass default; IE 7 more common; Vista retail available.

How a day felt

Boot XP · open IE · Digg / MySpace or Facebook · Flash YouTube · Gmail without begging for an invite. After March, tech friends won’t shut up about Twitter (“What are you doing?” · 140 chars). After May, you waste an hour with the Maps pegman in San Francisco or Vegas — and install a Facebook app that pokes all your friends. Late June (US): wait in line for an iPhone at $499 / $599 on Cingular/AT&T — full Safari, no App Store, EDGE when you’re not on Wi‑Fi. Fall: ad money boom · Beacon spoils a Christmas gift on your News Feed. Most of the world never touches an iPhone this year. The habit is still the laptop.
