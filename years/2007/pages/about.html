About 2007

2007 is the year the phone becomes a real web browser you carry — while most people still live on the desktop Web 2.0 stack. iPhone is announced Jan 9 and ships Jun 29. The browser is Safari. There is no App Store. Gmail opens worldwide Feb 14. Street View debuts May 29. Facebook Platform ships May 24. Twitter breaks out at SXSW. YouTube is Google-owned all year.

Scale (dual-cite — do not blend): Internet Live Stats June 121,892,559 websites (+43%) · 1,373,327,790 users · birthmark Tumblr.
NetCraft December class is about 155 million hostnames — that is December, not June. Shell here: Windows XP + IE 7. Vista is leftover retail, not the default desktop.

How a day felt

Boot XP. IE 7 has tabs now, and a phishing bar. The start page is still Yahoo-class: mail, news, weather, a search box you may not use. MySpace is still bigger than Facebook. Digg is the front page leftover from 2006. After dinner, YouTube is a Flash rectangle that buffers. In May you can drop a yellow figure on five American streets. In June someone in line has a phone that loads nytimes.com and it looks wrong — pinch is the joke, there is no app grid. Late year a $399 Kindle sells out, and a tumblelog is a small blog with less fuss.
