MARS PATHFINDER LANDS ON RED PLANET
July 4, 1997
Web posted at: 5:07 p.m. EDT (2107 GMT)

(CNN)— NASA's Mars Pathfinder spacecraft landed successfully on the surface of Mars on Friday, July 4th, and has begun transmitting extraordinary images of the Martian landscape at the Ares Vallis flood plain.

[NASA/JPL photo]
Sojourner rover on the Martian surface, July 1997

The small Sojourner rover — weighing just 23 pounds and roughly the size of a microwave oven — rolled off its ramp onto the rocky Martian soil on July 6th, making it the first wheeled vehicle to operate on another planet.

"This is a great day for the United States, a great day for NASA, and a great day for the people of the world," said NASA Administrator Daniel Goldin.

The landing marks the first time a spacecraft has successfully reached the Martian surface since the Viking missions in 1976 — a gap of 21 years.

* Web Record Shattered: NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory website was overwhelmed by public interest in the mission. The site recorded 46 million hits in a single day on July 8th — setting a Guinness World Record for Internet traffic and crashing mirror sites worldwide.

Pathfinder has transmitted more than 2.6 billion bits of information, including over 16,000 images from the lander and 550 images from Sojourner. The rover has analyzed several rocks using its Alpha Proton X-ray Spectrometer.

Scientists have been particularly excited by a rock they've named "Barnacle Bill," which appears to be more andesitic — similar to Earth rocks — than expected. "These rocks are telling us that Mars is more geologically diverse than we thought," said mission scientist Dr. Matthew Golombek.

The $265 million mission was designed as a technology demonstration under NASA's "faster, better, cheaper" philosophy championed by Administrator Goldin. The spacecraft used an innovative airbag landing system, bouncing 15 times before coming to rest.
