Microsoft Launches Internet Explorer 4.0
Posted by CmdrTaco on Wednesday October 01, @10:23AM
from the browser-wars dept.
Microsoft held a launch event in San Francisco for Internet Explorer 4.0, their latest assault on Netscape's browser dominance. The new version features Active Desktop integration, DHTML support, and the infamous stunt where Microsoft employees placed a giant "e" logo on Netscape's lawn. The browser wars are heating up.

Mars Pathfinder Sets Web Traffic Record
Posted by CmdrTaco on Friday July 04, @08:15AM
from the nasa-crashes-the-internet dept.
NASA's Mars Pathfinder mission generated 46 million hits in a single day on NASA's website, crashing servers around the world. The little Sojourner rover sent back images from the Martian surface and the whole planet wanted to see them. Mirror sites struggled to keep up with the demand.

New Search Projects at Stanford
Posted by CmdrTaco on Tuesday September 15, @03:42PM
from the future-of-search dept.
Two Stanford Ph.D. students, Larry Page and Sergey Brin, have registered the domain google.com. Their search engine project, originally called BackRub, uses a novel PageRank algorithm that analyzes link structures. Will this be the AltaVista killer? Time will tell.

AuctionWeb Becomes eBay
Posted by CmdrTaco on Monday September 01, @11:05AM
from the catchy-names dept.
Pierre Omidyar's online auction site AuctionWeb has officially rebranded as eBay. The site has been growing rapidly with users trading everything from Pez dispensers to electronics. The new name is a lot easier to remember, which should help with their expansion plans.

Deep Blue Defeats Kasparov — Machines Win
Posted by CmdrTaco on Sunday May 11, @06:30PM
from the rise-of-the-machines dept.
IBM's Deep Blue supercomputer has defeated world chess champion Garry Kasparov in a six-game match. This is the first time a computer has beaten a reigning world champion under standard chess tournament conditions. Kasparov accused IBM of cheating. The age of thinking machines is here.

HTML 4.0 Becomes W3C Recommendation
Posted by CmdrTaco on Thursday December 18, @09:12AM
from the standards-matter dept.
The W3C has published HTML 4.0 as an official Recommendation. The new spec includes better support for multimedia, scripting, style sheets, accessibility, and internationalization. Whether Netscape and Microsoft will actually implement it consistently remains to be seen.
