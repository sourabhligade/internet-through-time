About 2005

2005 is when Web 2.0 stops being a conference meme and becomes a business boom. Silicon Valley M&A returns; Ajax gets a name; Maps, YouTube, Reddit, and Digg reshape the everyday web; podcasting lands in iTunes; tech blogs invent the startup press beat.

Scale (Internet Live Stats, 2005): 64,780,617 websites (+26% from 2004) · ~1.027 billion internet users. Default shell here: Windows XP + IE 6. Cool bloggers use Firefox 1.x (~8% traffic share class, Pew Aug 2005) — not Chrome, not Vista.

Signature products (this immersion)

  • Google Maps — public Feb 8, 2005 · Ajax essay Feb 18 · API June · HousingMaps mashup ~April (pre-API)
  • YouTube — public beta Apr 23 · Me at the zoo · Broadcast Yourself · Sequoia $3.5M Nov · still independent all year
  • Reddit — Jun 23 · Y Combinator first class · boosts
  • Digg — public Dec 2004 · 2005 rise year · Diggnation ep.1 Jul 1
  • iTunes 4.9 podcasts — Jun 28 · 3,000+ free · >1M subscriptions in two days (Jun 30 PR)
  • TechCrunch — June · “Tracking Web 2.0”
  • Memeorandum — blog news clusters
  • Million Dollar Homepage — Aug 26 pixels
  • Skype — eBay deal Sep 12
  • Mashable · ProgrammableWeb — press / API culture
  • Google Video — Google’s 2005 video product (YouTube still independent)
  • del.icio.us — folksonomy · Yahoo Dec 9 (~300k users)

M&A wave (use exact dates)

  • Mar 20 — Yahoo acquires Flickr / Ludicorp (~$22–25M class) — not Yahoo Photos merge
  • Jul 18 — News Corp acquires Intermix / MySpace for $580M cash → Fox Interactive Media
  • Sep 12 — eBay acquires Skype ~$2.6B up-front
  • Dec 9 — Yahoo acquires del.icio.us (“fraternal twin Flickr”)

Social honesty

Facebook (Thefacebook rename era) is still network-gated: colleges, then high schools (Sep). Accel $12.7M in May. Not open to the entire internet (that is Sep 2006). No News Feed yet. MySpace is still the mass social product — and newly News Corp–owned.
