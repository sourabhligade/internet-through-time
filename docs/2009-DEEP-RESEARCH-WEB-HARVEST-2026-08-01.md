## 0. One-line thesis (locked)

**2009 is the year the social web goes mainstream and apps become daily habit:** **Twitter** leaves the geek corner (Oprah, Kutcher, Iran coverage lore); **Facebook** ships the **Like** button, buys **FriendFeed**, passes **MySpace**, and kills **Beacon**; **FarmVille** invents mass social-gaming spam energy; **iPhone 3GS** + **iPhone OS 3.0** + **50k+ apps / 1B+ downloads** make the App Store the default smartphone story; **Android** “comes of age” with many handsets; **Bing** challenges Google search branding; **Windows 7** rescues the PC desktop from Vista; **Kickstarter**, **Foursquare**, **WhatsApp**, and **UberCab** seed the 2010s — while most people still live on a **laptop** with **IE 8 / Firefox / Chrome Windows**.

### 1.2 Daily ritual (exhibit voice)

Boot **Windows XP** (still mass early year) or try **Windows 7** after **October** · open **IE 7 → IE 8** (or Firefox, or that new **Chrome**) · check **Facebook** (Like everything) · open **Twitter** if you’re Oprah-curious · **YouTube** after dinner · **Gmail** always on · friends with **iPhone 3GS** talk about **cut/copy/paste** and **video** · play **FarmVille** until 2 a.m. · geeks try **Bing** for a week · Austin kids **check in** on **Foursquare** · Europe still has **Spotify** invites · most of the world still fat laptop, but the **phone in the pocket** finally runs *apps every day*.

### 1.3 Macro backdrop (mood, not product spine)

- Global **financial crisis hangover** — startups lean lean; freemium / ad / VC “Web 2.0 capitalism” hardens.  
- **Content farms** (Demand Media class) industrialize SEO sludge — early warning for search quality wars.  
- **Real-time web** is the buzzword (ReadWriteWeb summit culture · Cybercultural).  
- **Iran June 2009** election protests become Western media’s “Twitter Revolution” story (overstated as organizer tool; real as **global attention / witness** channel).  
- PC is **not dead** — Windows 7 is a genuine mass OS comeback story late year.
