## 0. One-line thesis

**2002 is the year the always-on web and the social web start to feel inevitable:** Pew finds **~21% of U.S. internet users** on home broadband (up from ~6% in 2000) — the net shifts from a “sometimes” tool to an **always-on appliance**. Design splits: **Flash/Shockwave** microsites and streaming portals (MTV) still define cool, while **tableless CSS** hits mainstream via **Wired’s Oct 2002 redesign**. The **blogosphere** becomes a media ecosystem (Hiler / Microcontent News; Movable Type trackbacks; blogrolls + RSS). After Napster’s legal death, **KaZaA / Morpheus / Grokster / Gnutella clients** own the music wild west; Apple ships **iPod gen 2** and begins the long courtship of labels for a future store (not yet iTunes Music Store). **Friendster** launches (founding/public story **2002**) as the first mass “friend graph” rehearsal before MySpace (2003). Browsers: **IE6 near ~90%** monopoly sleep; **Mozilla 1.0** (Jun 5) and **Phoenix 0.1** (Sep 23 — Firefox ancestor) plant open-source hope. Mood = **creative, chatty, slightly lawless** — not Facebook, not the iTunes Store, not Web 2.0 branding.
