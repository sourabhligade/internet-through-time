## 0. One-line thesis

**1999 is the year the open web’s next chapters appear while the browser war ends:** Microsoft ships **IE5** and finishes burying Netscape; **Napster** invents mass P2P music culture; **Blogger** + the word “blog” invent mainstream personal publishing; **Google** takes real VC money and starts to be *recommended* (not yet default); **Yahoo buys GeoCities**; Amazon becomes the multi-category “king of cybercommerce” (Bezos *Time* Person of the Year); the **dot-com bubble** is near peak; **Y2K** anxiety fills news and IT theater. The room should feel **louder, richer, and more anxious** than 1998 — and introduce **file-sharing + blogs** as the new rituals.

This year is **not** yet the crash (that’s 2000–2001), **not** broadband-as-default, and **not** Amazon smile / modern Google monopoly UI.

## 12. Accuracy pitfalls (print next to keyboard)

1. **IE5 is the default** — 1998 room was IE4; don’t ship 1999 still looking like IE4-only.  
2. **Napster is a desktop client story** — not a Spotify clone, not 2001 courtroom endgame.  
3. **Google is rising, not ruling** — portals and AltaVista still matter.  
4. **eBay goes multicolor** — the black-only ban applied to 1997–98; reverse it carefully.  
5. **Amazon smile is still forbidden** — expansion yes, smile no.  
6. **Blogger ≠ WordPress** — form + FTP mental model.  
7. **Y2K is anxiety theater**, not “the internet broke.”  
8. Prefer **Wayback 1999** over inventing Flash intros for every site.  
9. **No real MP3s** in the Napster reconstruction.  
10. AOL–Netscape **closes** in 1999; announcement was 1998.
