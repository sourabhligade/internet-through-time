## 0. The feeling of the year (one paragraph)

**2007 still lives on a fat laptop.** You boot **Windows XP**, open **IE 6 or 7**, and the day is Digg → MySpace or Facebook → YouTube (Flash) → maybe Gmail (now *without* begging for an invite). In March, tech people won’t shut up about **Twitter** after **SXSW** plasma screens. In May you **add SuperPoke** on Facebook and drag the little **pegman** on Google Maps into **Street View** for five US cities only. Late June you wait in line for an **iPhone** that costs **$499/$599**, talks only on **Cingular/AT&T**, and browses the *real* web in **Safari** — but there is **no App Store**, so “apps” means web bookmarks and maybe a jailbreak rumor. Fall is ad money and privacy fights (**Beacon** spoils Christmas gifts on your News Feed). November: Google says **Android** is coming — *not* a Gphone yet. Most of the world never touches an iPhone this year. The miracle is that a phone *could* be a real browser; the habit is still desktop Web 2.0.

## 2. A day on the 2007 internet (persona rituals)

Use these as **tour trails** and “Starting Point” sidebars — they are how people *actually* spent hours.

### 2.1 High-school / college (US-ish)

1. Homeroom: check **MySpace** bulletins · Top 8 drama · HTML profile bling (glitter GIFs, autoplay song).  
2. Lunch: **AIM** away messages (“at lunch brb”) — still cultural even as SMS rises.  
3. After school: **YouTube** rabbit holes (Flash, 240p–360p feel) · **Digg** front page · maybe **Facebook** if college network / open account.  
4. Night: **iTunes** library + 99¢ Store · **Netflix** red envelope on the counter · **World of Warcraft** / **Steam** lore if gamer.  
5. Late year: friend texts “iPhone line at the mall” — you go look, or watch keynote clips on YouTube.

### 2.2 Knowledge worker / blogger

1. Boot XP · **Outlook** or **Gmail** (open signup is a *gift* after years of invites).  
2. **Google Reader** / **Bloglines** for RSS · **TechCrunch / Mashable / RWW**.  
3. **Docs** share a spreadsheet · **Maps** for a meeting · after May: waste 20 minutes in **Street View**.  
4. **Twitter** “What are you doing?” from a third-party client (Twitterrific class) — API culture.  
5. **Facebook** Platform apps spam your feed with quizzes · you install one anyway.

### 2.3 Tech-conference person (March SXSW slice)

1. Hallway **60″ plasma** scrolling tweets.  
2. SMS shortcode **40404** lore · “where’s the party?” in 140 chars.  
3. Dodgeball vs Twitter conversations · award speech short.  
4. Fly home · teach coworkers to join · “it sounds stupid until you’re there.”

### 2.4 Late-adopter “I just want email”

1. Still **Yahoo Mail / Hotmail** · maybe finally creates **Gmail** after Valentine’s open day.  
2. **Yahoo** portal homepage habit.  
3. **eBay + PayPal** · **Amazon** smile.  
4. Heard of Facebook from a niece · still thinks MySpace is “the internet.”  
5. Vista came with a new PC and they hate **UAC** prompts.

## 4. Period language cheat sheet (write like 2007)

### 4.1 Words people said

| Use | Avoid (anachronism) |
|-----|---------------------|
| Web 2.0 · user-generated content · mashup | “creator economy” as default |
| Social networking site / SNS | “the app” as only metaphor |
| Blogosphere · RSS · feed reader | Modern “newsletter Substack default” as only story |
| Digg it · bury · Top 8 · bulletins | “algorithmic For You feed” as 2007 default |
| Tweet / Twitter / update · 140 | “post on X” |
| Friend · poke · app · News Feed | Reels · Stories as default |
| Instant messaging · AIM · away message | Discord as period default |
| Flash video · embed code | HTML5-only video lore |
| Broadband · always-on · Wi-Fi | 5G |
| Jailbreak (whisper) · unlock | App Store side-loading story as official 2007 |
| EDGE · Wi-Fi for iPhone data | “unlimited LTE” |
| Beta (Gmail still wears it) | “GA forever polished SaaS” |

### 4.2 UI chrome voice

- **IE:** blue/gray toolbar · Favorites · status bar “Done” · ActiveX warnings  
- **XP:** green Start · Luna blue · system tray  
- **Google family:** sparse white · blue links · Ajax without full reload  
- **Facebook:** blue header · left nav · app canvas  
- **MySpace:** chaos HTML · tables · autoplay  
- **YouTube:** white/gray · star ratings era class · related column  
- **Twitter:** sparse · character counter · “update” button  

### 4.3 Microcopy patterns (exhibit-safe)

- “Add to queue” (Netflix)  
- “Digg it” / “bury”  
- “What are you doing?”  
- “Share with friends” (pre-Beacon innocence → post-Beacon suspicion)  
- “No invitation required” (Gmail open)  
- “View larger map” / “Street View”  
- “Install application” (FB Platform)  
- “Skip intro” / “Buffering 0:00 / 3:42”

## 7. Exhibit copy blocks (ready for about / home / cool)

### 7.1 Thesis (short)

> 2007 is when a phone finally browses the *real* web — and when platforms learn to be platforms. Most people still live on XP, IE, Digg, MySpace/Facebook, and Flash YouTube. The iPhone ships without an App Store. Gmail stops being an exclusive invite club. Street View puts five cities under your cursor. Facebook lets third parties build apps. Twitter stops being a toy at SXSW.

### 7.2 Scale one-liner

> About **122 million** websites and **1.37 billion** users (Internet Live Stats, June class) — up roughly **43%** sites from 2006. Tumblr is the Live Stats “birthmark” launch.

### 7.3 Ban callout (always nearby)

> **Not yet:** Chrome browser · App Store · mass Android phones · modern iOS home · modern X · Gmail-as-invite-only year story.

### 7.4 Privacy one-liner

> Late 2007, Facebook Beacon teaches a generation that “share with friends” can mean “share with advertisers — and your aunt.”

## 11. What “awesome relatable content” means for implementers

1. **Lead with a human beat**, then the product: line outside the Apple Store → then Safari specs.  
2. **Keep contradictions:** MySpace larger than Facebook *and* Platform is the future; Vista ships *and* XP stays; iPhone miracle *and* EDGE sucks.  
3. **Year-lock every sentence:** if it needs Chrome or App Store, it is 2008.  
4. **Prefer period phrases** from §4 over modern product marketing.  
5. **Connect rooms** (trails) — people did not live in single-site silos.  
6. **Never invent logos** — text and structure over fake brand pixels.
